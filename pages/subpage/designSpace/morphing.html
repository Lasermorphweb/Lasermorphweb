Design Space / Morphing

Morphing

Morphing is an optional design strategy based on four material-state transitions. The measurements below describe the tested specimens and should not be treated as universal constants.

Morphing Cycle

The sequence connects compressed, expanded, dry, and recompressed states. Dry-state thickness retention was tested on 12 specimens for 15 reuse cycles; the overall mean at cycle 15 was 84.4% ± 5.1% relative to each specimen’s first hydration–drying baseline. These data do not establish full-shape fidelity, a long-term plateau, or fatigue resistance.

Morphing Cycle

Recompression

Heat at 120°C with pressure returns the dried object to a compact sheet. Manual compression is less uniform and leaves a thicker sheet. Repeated cycles can reduce retained thickness.

Recompression

Drying and Hardening

Drying hardens the material and changes its geometry. Across six thickness groups, mean first-drying shrinkage ranged from 17.4% to 32.0% of hydrated thickness. The roughly 30% value in the overview diagram is an illustration, not a universal constant.

Drying and Hardening

Sealing

A physical barrier such as plastic film slows evaporation and prolongs the wet, compliant state. This supports temporary shape maintenance.

Sealing

Hydration-driven Expansion

Room-temperature water triggers rapid thickness recovery. Across 35 specimens in six thickness groups, mean hydrated thickness ratios ranged from 8.39 to 13.30 relative to initial thickness. Calibrate by thickness group and material batch.
