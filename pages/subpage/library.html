Shapes

Level 1: Slopes

Varying engraving depth produces local height differences and inclined surfaces, providing the basis for further geometric control.

Level 2: Curved Surfaces / 2.5D

Creating raised features and curved surfaces, including concave and convex forms, cylinders, and cones, through programmed local thickness recovery.

Level 3: 3D

Approximating continuous 3D volumes such as bears, doughnuts, turtles, and simplified vehicle or bunny models. The goal is overall form, rather than exact reproduction of fine detail.

Scalability

Single-sided

Engraving one face of the compressed sheet to create height fields, textures, reliefs, and approximate volumetric forms.

Double-sided

Engraving front and back faces to produce more complete volumetric forms. Flipping requires careful front-to-back positioning and alignment.

Side-plane

Engraving side surfaces to extend the geometry achievable from a single face. Reorient the material and verify settings for the chosen processing conditions.

Six-sided

Combining front, back, and side-surface engraving to distribute geometric features across all six faces, with alignment maintained between orientations.

Horizontal Joining

Joining modules in the X–Y plane using interlocking features to build objects larger than the fabrication bed. Account for expansion and drying when sizing joints.

Vertical Joining

Stacking and joining modules vertically to create structures taller than a single fabrication setup allows. Check joint fit after hydration and drying.

Resolution

Vertical Precision

Vertical height differences are reproducible at approximately the 1.5 mm scale under the tested conditions. Fiber variability still causes local differences; this is not a minimum dry ablation depth.

Surface Quality

Cross-scanning with orthogonal paths reduces ridge-like protrusions and improves smoothness. Local thermal effects and the cellulose structure also influence surface quality.

Structural Limits

Sample tests suggest a minimum wall thickness around 0.8 mm to reduce collapse or tearing risk. This is a practical guideline, not guaranteed stability; verify thin walls with a test sample.

Typography

Readable character height is approximately 0.75–1.0 cm after expansion. At least 1.0 cm is recommended in practice; legibility depends on the font, material, and processing conditions.

Morphing

Morphing

Water triggers thickness recovery (group means 8.39–13.30×). Sealing slows evaporation; drying hardens the form with 17.4–32.0% mean thickness shrinkage across groups. Recompression at 120°C supports repeated deployment, with some thickness loss over reuse.
